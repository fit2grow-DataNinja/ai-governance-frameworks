# Phase 2 — Review of selected examples

**Status:** Completed  
**Reviewed:** 23 November 2026

This note records a small, bounded review of published NIST AI RMF use cases and European Commission AI literacy practices. The examples are learning inputs, not endorsed controls, legal determinations, or evidence that another organization is compliant.

## Cross-case observations

### 1. Framework use is contextual

The two NIST examples apply the same voluntary framework to different organizational settings and purposes. San José reports a portfolio-level review of existing public-sector governance; Workday reports mapping a company control framework and product/third-party risk activities. These are examples of possible application, not a universal sequence or template.

### 2. Governance connects roles, processes, and evidence

Across the cases, the described work includes named organizational roles or cross-functional groups, procurement/product review, evaluation, feedback, and monitoring. These mechanisms may inform future method design. A project record still needs to identify which mechanisms actually exist, who owns them, and what evidence supports their status.

### 3. Literacy is one part of enablement, and should fit the audience and context

The two Commission examples describe different combinations of broad foundations, role-specific or advanced learning, practical sessions, peer support, feedback, and multi-format materials. This supports exploring AI literacy as an organizational capability and enablement topic. It does not mean either example is a required program design or a complete organizational-readiness model.

### 4. Reported outcomes need careful handling

The examples report self-assessments, reach, survey feedback, participation, and usage measures. The records capture these as source claims. The underlying methods, baselines, and independent validation are not sufficiently described to treat the reported results as proof of effectiveness or transferable benchmarks.

## Decisions for repository materials

- **NIST orientation:** No change. It already describes the four functions, iterative use, and the non-endorsement limit for use cases.
- **EU AI Act matrix:** Made one targeted correction to the Article 4 checklist during source review: replaced the older “sufficient AI literacy” phrasing with the amended Article 4 duty to take measures supporting development of literacy and added the express statement that providers/deployers need not guarantee an individual literacy level. No new obligation screen or broader matrix restructuring was needed.
- **Project-screening template:** No structural change. Article 4 remains a separate screen for project-specific application.
- **Source register:** Add individual records for each example substantively reviewed, plus an Article 4 record.
- **AI Enablement Method / AI Organizational Readiness:** These cases may be useful as learning inputs for future work on role-specific literacy, cross-functional ownership, feedback, and evidence. Any transfer should be documented in those repositories and distinguished from legal requirements.

## Review limits

NIST says it does not validate or endorse individual organizations or approaches in its case collection. The Commission says its literacy collection supports learning and exchange and that copying a practice does not automatically create a presumption of Article 4 compliance. These are organization-reported accounts; this review did not independently verify their implementation or outcomes.

The review is deliberately narrow. It does not determine whether the examples are representative, test the effectiveness of any practice, or assess any organization’s compliance.
