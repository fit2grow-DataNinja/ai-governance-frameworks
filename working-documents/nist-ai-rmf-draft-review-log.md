# NIST AI RMF working-document draft review log

**Review date:** 24 November 2026  
**Framework baseline checked:** NIST AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 100-1 (2023)  
**Review scope:** Source fidelity and usability of the NIST outcome inventory, application matrix, and project assessment record.

## Review outcome

The first drafts are suitable for a bounded company-document scan and use-case application, subject to their stated draft status and limitations. No material change to the Core outcome structure was needed in this review. The assessment-record example row was corrected so its cells match the expanded Main focus table.

## Source-fidelity review

- The [Core outcome inventory](nist-ai-rmf-core-outcome-inventory.md) and [application matrix](nist-ai-rmf-application-matrix-draft.md) cover the four NIST AI RMF 1.0 functions and all 72 subcategories (GOVERN 19, MAP 18, MEASURE 22, MANAGE 13).
- The matrix prompts are repository-authored summaries, not NIST text. They point readers to the official Core for full outcome language.
- The inventory accurately distinguishes Core outcomes from voluntary, non-exhaustive Playbook suggestions.
- Profile tailoring, use of existing published NIST profiles before project-specific tailoring, and Current/Target Profile comparison are distinguished from the repository’s own workflow. The profile search method, main-focus/sub-focus tiers, disposition labels, evidence taxonomy, and form fields are explicitly repository-created.
- The record’s full-coverage disposition convention (one disposition per Core subcategory) is a portfolio design choice. It does not imply NIST requires every organization to assess every subcategory.
- Current states are evidence-based descriptions; targets are developed with relevant company representatives, with proposed/agreed/approved status recorded. This collaboration and approval workflow is a repository method, not a NIST-prescribed procedure.
- The drafts do not use a NIST-mandated score, maturity scale, universal evidence package, or compliance determination.

## Usability review

- The matrix provides a consistent prompt for each Core subcategory; the record keeps context-specific answers, source references, evidence limits, decisions, and actions.
- Main focus points and Sub-focus points provide an overview without leaving selected outcomes unassessed. Other Core outcomes have a separate disposition and rationale area.
- Profile search and reuse decisions precede project-specific tailoring; the record has fields for source/status, scope, fit, decision, and gaps.
- The assessment record now captures Current Profile state, Target Profile state, company participants and roles, decision owner, approval status, and unresolved disagreement.
- A coverage check totals all 72 outcomes and verifies that each selected outcome appears in one focus group.

## Remaining boundaries

This review checks the draft structures and representative prompts against the official source basis. It does not validate the tools through an organizational deployment, prove that the forms are complete for every sector, or claim that a company’s controls operate effectively. The company-document scan should be used to identify usability improvements and recurring artifact intents within its stated sample only.
