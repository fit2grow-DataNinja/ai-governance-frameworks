## Relevance

- Why it is relevant to this repository or project: The Commission page describes a different organization and training approach, but does not publish reusable training or assessment materials. It is not a working-document source for the repository’s templates.

## What the source says

The Commission page identifies OpenSky as a medium-sized Irish ICT company and AI provider and deployer. The reported program includes a Foundations track for all staff and applied labs/bootcamps for engineers and analysts, covering topics such as safe use, privacy/security, bias, evaluation, guardrails, data handling, integration, and reliability. A Data & AI Centre of Excellence coordinates the program with HR support; completion records and staff feedback are also described. OpenSky reports workshops for clients in public, health, and pharmaceutical sectors.

The page reports increases in employee training participation and use of specific AI tools, plus positive client feedback. It also describes uneven departmental engagement and client concerns about data breaches and control of AI decisions.

## Evidence and limits

- Actions, controls, or evidence described by the source: Role-oriented curricula, delivery formats, completion tracking, recurring feedback, client workshops, and reported participation/tool-use/client-feedback KPIs.
- What the source does not establish: It does not define KPI baselines, denominators, survey methods, causal attribution, independent validation, or whether the measures establish Article 4 compliance.
- Scope, assumptions, or limitations: The page is an organizational report in a Commission learning collection. It gives historical rollout dates and future plans, but no publication/update date is displayed; current completion or effectiveness is not independently verified.
- Does the source state whether the example is validated, endorsed, or illustrative? The Commission collection is for learning and exchange; it states that replicating a practice does not automatically create a presumption of Article 4 compliance.

## Our analysis

- Possible relevance to a project or reusable document: The practice page is a descriptive summary and does not provide a reusable training curriculum, assessment form, or operating procedure. Do not derive template fields from its generic statements.
- Adaptations required and rationale: Select content from the actual system, tasks, data, risks, role, and audience. Treat the reported outcome percentages as claims from the page, not as benchmarks or proven effects.
- Open questions: What exact baselines, time periods, and methods underlie the reported KPI changes? The page does not provide enough detail to reproduce or independently assess them.
- Confidence: High that this summarizes the published page; low for interpreting the percentages as causal or generalizable evidence.
