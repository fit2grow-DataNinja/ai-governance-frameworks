## Relevance

- Claim or topic being investigated: How an AI deployer reports organizing literacy and enablement across employee groups.
- Exact provision, framework section, or example: VERBUND’s reported literacy program in the context of AI Act Article 4
- Why it is relevant to this repository or project: It connects role-specific learning, organizational coordination, distributed support, and accessibility of learning materials—topics relevant to later AI enablement and readiness work.

## What the source says

The Commission page identifies VERBUND as a large Austrian energy company and AI deployer. It describes monthly learning sessions, mandatory e-training, Basics and Advanced courses, design sprints, role-specific tracks, an AI Academy in development, and AI Champions. It says the AI Center of Enablement brings together HR, Communications, IT, and business units.

The page reports that the initiative reached nearly 30% of the total workforce and more than half of office-based employees, using attendance tracking and post-session surveys. It also reports that voluntary sessions leave some groups unengaged and that the organization plans to consolidate and expand activities through the AI Academy.

## Evidence and limits

- Actions, controls, or evidence described by the source: Training formats, cross-functional ownership, role-targeted learning, peer support, accessible multi-format materials, attendance tracking, and post-session survey feedback.
- What the source does not establish: It does not provide underlying attendance data, survey design or response rates, a baseline, independent evaluation, or evidence that the program establishes Article 4 compliance.
- Scope, assumptions, or limitations: The page reports the organization’s practice through a Commission collection. Its publication/update date is not shown; plans and reported reach should not be treated as independently verified current status.
- Does the source state whether the example is validated, endorsed, or illustrative? The Commission collection is for learning and exchange; it states that replicating a practice does not automatically create a presumption of Article 4 compliance.

## Our analysis

- Possible relevance to a project or reusable document: Illustrates how AI literacy can be treated as a coordinated organizational enablement activity rather than a single generic training event.
- Adaptations required and rationale: Tailor audience, role tracks, content, format, ownership, and feedback to the actual systems and people in scope. Do not treat the reported reach or program design as a compliance threshold.
- Open questions: How are learning needs identified for each AI system and affected role? How do attendance or survey signals lead to program changes?
- Confidence: High that this summarizes the published page; limited confidence in effectiveness and compliance conclusions because those are not established by the page.
