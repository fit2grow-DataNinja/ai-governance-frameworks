## Relevance

- Claim or topic being investigated: How an AI provider reports mapping its existing governance arrangements to the NIST AI RMF and embedding it in product and third-party risk processes.
- Exact provision, framework section, or example: Workday’s reported mapping of its common control framework and related product/third-party risk work
- Why it is relevant to this repository or project: The Workday case reports links between its framework mapping, product evaluation, roles, and third-party assessment, but the underlying materials are not included in the case document.

## What the source says

Workday reports mapping its common control framework to AI RMF categories and subcategories, identifying existing controls and areas for refinement. It says its responsible-AI guidelines, product risk evaluation, and third-party risk questionnaire are anchored in the AI RMF. It describes collaboration across Product & Technology, Responsible AI, Product Legal, and Privacy & Engineering, along with an AI Advisory Board and plans for templates and standard operating procedures.

Workday describes itself as an AI provider/developer and notes that when it uses AI tools it developed or procured from a third party and interacts with end users, it acts as a deployer.

## Evidence and limits

- Actions, controls, or evidence described by the source: The case describes a framework-to-control mapping, cross-functional roles, a product-risk process, third-party questionnaires, an advisory board, and planned operational documents.
- What the source does not establish: It does not provide independent evaluation, detailed outcome measures, or evidence sufficient to establish the effectiveness of the reported controls.
- Scope, assumptions, or limitations: The account is published by Workday and is a high-level organizational case, not a technical implementation guide or legal determination. Some activities are described as ongoing or planned.
- Does the source state whether the example is validated, endorsed, or illustrative? NIST explicitly says it does not validate or endorse the individual organizations or approaches in its use-case collection.

## Our analysis

- Possible relevance to a project or reusable document: The case summary does not provide the control mapping, risk-evaluation artifact, questionnaire, procedures, or evidence needed to reproduce the reported approach. Do not derive template fields from the summary.
- Adaptations required and rationale: Start from the actual use case, roles, risks, and evidence. A portfolio project can borrow the mapping discipline but should not claim Workday’s governance structures or scale.
- Open questions: What evidence demonstrates control operation or outcomes, and how are findings used to change controls? The case does not provide enough detail to answer.
- Confidence: High that this record reflects the published account; limited confidence in any effectiveness claim because the source does not independently validate outcomes.
