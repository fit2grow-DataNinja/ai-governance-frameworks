## Relevance

- Claim or topic being investigated: What Article 4 requires and what the Commission literacy examples can—and cannot—teach.
- Exact provision, framework section, or example: Article 4(1)–(3)
- Why it is relevant to this repository or project: It is the binding legal anchor for the selected AI literacy practices and for the matrix’s separate AI literacy screen.

## What the source says

Article 4(1) requires providers and deployers to take measures to support the AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on their behalf. Measures must take account of those people’s technical knowledge, experience, education and training; the context in which the systems are used; and the people or groups on whom they are used. The provision expressly says this does not require a provider or deployer to guarantee a specific AI-literacy level for any individual.

Article 4(2) assigns the Commission and Member States support roles, including publishing practical examples. Article 4(3) provides for Board recommendations.

## Evidence and limits

- Actions, controls, or evidence described by the source: The legal text establishes a duty to take context-sensitive measures; it does not prescribe a single training format.
- What the source does not establish: It does not determine which measures are sufficient for a particular organization or prove that a listed practice satisfies the obligation.
- Scope, assumptions, or limitations: The EUR-Lex Regulation is the legal source. The Service Desk’s explanatory summary is explicitly non-binding. Applicability and measures require facts about the provider/deployer, people, systems, and use contexts.
- Does the source state whether the example is validated, endorsed, or illustrative? Not applicable to the legal provision. The Commission’s separate practice collection is for learning and exchange and states that replication does not automatically create a presumption of compliance.

## Our analysis

- Possible relevance to a project or reusable document: Use as the legal anchor when considering literacy measures; design those measures from the organization’s roles, relevant people, actual AI systems and contexts, and affected groups.
- Adaptations required and rationale: Treat training examples as possible inputs to a tailored program, not as a ready-made compliance checklist.
- Open questions: How to operationalize evidence of measures proportionately for a particular project or organization; the Act does not prescribe a universal evidence package.
- Confidence: High for the cited text; case-specific application remains outside this record.
