## Relevance

- Claim or topic being investigated: How a public-sector organization can use the AI RMF to review an existing AI governance program and identify capability gaps.
- Exact provision, framework section, or example: City of San José’s self-assessment against the AI RMF’s four functions and 72 subcomponents
- Why it is relevant to this repository or project: The NIST case PDF reports organizational governance and readiness work around a portfolio of AI systems, including procured systems. For this project, the report is context only: it does not include the underlying assessment worksheet or evidence. The PDF mentions a separate City AI Review Framework, which has not been examined in this record.

## What the source says

The City reports that it reviewed its existing governance against the AI RMF, rated all 72 subcomponents on a four-point maturity scale, and identified gaps. The reported existing arrangements include a centralized IT governance owner, a working group and advisory taskforce, procurement review, initial risk analysis, impact assessment for mid/high-risk proposals, data-use protocols, a public algorithm register for approved systems affecting the public, and ongoing monitoring for high-risk systems.

The case identifies further needs in formal policy, staff education, mechanisms for end-user feedback, and evaluation procedures such as robustness, drift, stress testing, error documentation, and decommissioning. The City notes that most of its AI systems were procured rather than developed internally.

## Evidence and limits

- Actions, controls, or evidence described by the source: The case describes governance structures and processes, a self-scoring exercise, average scores by RMF function, identified gaps, and planned next steps.
- What the source does not establish: It does not show that every proposed action was later implemented or independently assess whether the self-scores or governance outcomes were accurate or effective.
- Scope, assumptions, or limitations: This is the City’s reported self-assessment, not an external audit. The PDF’s action plans refer to 2024–2025; their present status is not established by this source.
- Does the source state whether the example is validated, endorsed, or illustrative? NIST explicitly says it does not validate or endorse the individual organizations or approaches in its use-case collection.

## Our analysis

- Possible relevance to a project or reusable document: The reviewed case PDF is not a reusable assessment method. Do not derive template fields from its generic summary. If the separately mentioned City AI Review Framework is later selected, review it as a distinct source and decide independently whether it contains reusable working material.
- Adaptations required and rationale: For a bounded use-case, scale the questions to the system and affected people; do not copy the City’s risk labels, scoring, or governance structure as a universal model.
- Open questions: What changes were implemented after the assessment, and how were they evaluated? This PDF does not answer those questions.
- Confidence: High that the record accurately summarizes the published case; low for claims about effectiveness beyond what the case reports.
