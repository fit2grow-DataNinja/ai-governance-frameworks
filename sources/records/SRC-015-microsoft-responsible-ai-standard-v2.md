## What the source says

The standard calls for impact assessment early in development, review by identified reviewers and approvals before development starts, and updates/reviews at least annually, when intended uses are added, and before moving to a new release stage. Other goals address restricted/sensitive uses, fitness for purpose, stakeholder oversight, transparency, reliability/safety, privacy, and security.

## Evidence and limits

- Actions, controls, or evidence described by the source: Published internal requirements for impact assessment, review, approval, and review/update triggers.
- What the source does not establish: It does not provide completed assessments for particular systems in the passages reviewed, or independently establish consistent implementation or effectiveness.
- Scope, assumptions, or limitations: The standard is Microsoft’s published account of its own requirements. Its scope and applicability are defined by Microsoft; this is not a universal organizational requirement.
- Does the source state whether the example is validated, endorsed, or illustrative? It is Microsoft’s own standard, not an independently validated example.

## Our analysis

- Possible relevance to a project or reusable document: Documentary alignment with MAP 5.1 for impact assessment, GOVERN 1.5 for review cadence/triggers, and partial alignment with GOVERN 2.1 for named reviewers/approvals.
- Adaptations required and rationale: Use as an example of artifact intent and content. Do not copy its requirements as if they were NIST or universal requirements. The crosswalk should cite exact requirement and page; a standard is evidence of a documented policy/requirement, not proof of performance.
- Open questions: How the standard is implemented in specific product teams and what completed assessment evidence is available publicly.
- Confidence: High that the source contains the stated requirements; no conclusion about actual implementation.
