# NIST AI Risk Management Framework

**Working orientation — last reviewed 24 November 2026**

## What it is

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) is a voluntary framework for organizing management of risks associated with AI systems across design, development, deployment, use, and evaluation. The current published core is AI RMF 1.0; NIST’s AI Resource Center says that version is being revised, so check the official resource center for updates.

## Core functions

| Function | Working question |
|---|---|
| **Govern** | What organizational policies, responsibilities, culture, and oversight support AI risk management? |
| **Map** | What is the system’s context, intended use, affected parties, and risk landscape? |
| **Measure** | How will risks and system behavior be analyzed, assessed, tested, and tracked? |
| **Manage** | How will identified risks be prioritized, treated, monitored, and responded to? |

The functions connect and can be revisited iteratively; they are not a one-time linear checklist.

The Playbook offers voluntary suggestions aligned to Core outcomes. NIST describes it as a companion resource, not a complete checklist or mandatory sequence. Profiles adapt the RMF to a use case, technology, or sector. NIST does not validate or endorse the individual approaches shown in its use-case collection.

## How this repository uses NIST

Use the RMF to structure project questions, decisions, and evidence. For a portfolio project, identify relevant outcomes, explain why they matter in context, and link any claimed control to project evidence. Mapping to the RMF does not mean certification or compliance.

## Planned NIST working documents

The repository has first drafts of a traceable application matrix and project assessment record. These repository-created learning aids retain all 72 Core subcategories as a reference catalog and require a project-specific disposition for each. Selected outcomes are grouped into **Main focus points** (most central to the project context, material risks, and decisions) and **Sub-focus points** (supporting or qualifying outcomes); remaining outcomes are recorded as not selected, deferred, unable to assess, or not applicable, with rationale or information gaps. The focus tiers are an overview aid created for this portfolio, not NIST rankings or requirements. They do not replace or reduce assessment of the selected outcomes. The documents are not NIST-prescribed checklists. The [development approach](../../working-documents/nist-ai-rmf-documentation-development-approach.md) defines the research, mapping, traceability, and maintenance steps. The [Core outcome inventory](../../working-documents/nist-ai-rmf-core-outcome-inventory.md) indexes all 72 AI RMF 1.0 subcategories and records the source baseline. Review the [application matrix draft](../../working-documents/nist-ai-rmf-application-matrix-draft.md) and [project assessment record template](../../templates/nist-ai-rmf-project-assessment-record.template.md). See [SRC-013](../../sources/records/SRC-013-nist-ai-rmf-core-and-profiles.md) and [SRC-014](../../sources/records/SRC-014-nist-ai-rmf-playbook.md) for the reviewed source basis.
