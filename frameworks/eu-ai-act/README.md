# EU Artificial Intelligence Act

**Working orientation — legal source snapshot checked 19 November 2026**

## What it is

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 establishes harmonised rules for AI systems and general-purpose AI models in the European Union. Obligations depend on the system’s intended purpose, use context, risk classification, and the operator’s role. A project should not be classified by a generic risk score alone.

## Four-category orientation

The Commission describes four broad categories: prohibited (unacceptable) practices, high-risk systems, specific transparency-risk cases, and minimal/no-risk systems. These categories are a useful orientation; the specific legal tests and obligations are set in the Regulation.

1. Screen for practices prohibited by **Article 5**.
2. Evaluate high-risk classification under **Article 6**, including routes in **Annex I** and **Annex III**, and any applicable filter or transition.
3. Check specific transparency obligations under **Article 50**.
4. If none of those specific obligations apply, still check other relevant law, especially data-protection and sectoral rules.

## Role matters

Identify whether the organization acts as a **provider**, **deployer**, or in more than one role. High-risk provider obligations and deployer obligations differ. Use the current Regulation and actual facts for role analysis.

## High-risk requirements in brief

For applicable high-risk systems, the Regulation includes requirements concerning risk management, data and data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, transparency and information for deployers, human oversight, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, quality management, conformity assessment, registration, and post-market monitoring. Applicability and responsibility depend on the legal provision and system context.

**Annex IV** sets technical-documentation elements for applicable high-risk systems. **Article 27** requires a fundamental-rights impact assessment only for specified deployers and high-risk systems; it is not universal. A GDPR DPIA is a separate question under data-protection law.

## Interpretation and applicability caveat

This page is an educational orientation, not a legal opinion. It does not determine whether a specific project is in scope, high-risk, or compliant. Verify current law, implementation guidance, and application dates for the specific system and market before making decisions.
